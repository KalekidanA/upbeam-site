How to set multiple alarms on iPhone (and turn them all off at once)

6 min read · Updated November 2026

The short version
  • In the Clock app, tap Alarms, then the + button, once for each alarm you want. Use Repeat and Label so you know which is which.
  • To switch them all off, there's no single button: ask Siri to turn off all your alarms, or make a three-step Shortcut.
  • Fewer, better alarms usually beat a long string of them. One alarm at your real get-up time, plus one backup if you need it, is a good target.

Lots of people stack alarms: 6:30, 6:35, 6:40, "REALLY get up." Others need different alarms for work days, gym days and weekends. The iPhone's Clock app handles all of this, but it isn't obvious how to keep a long list tidy, and there's no "turn everything off" switch for the day you get to sleep in. This guide covers both, for iPhones on iOS 26 and later.

How to set multiple alarms in the Clock app

Each alarm is created separately. Repeat these steps for every alarm you want:

  1. Open the Clock app and tap the Alarms tab.
  2. Tap the + (Add) button.
  3. Set the time.
  4. Choose your options: Repeat (which days it rings), Label (its name), Sound and Snooze.
  5. Tap Done.

All your alarms then sit in one list, each with its own switch. Tap the switch to turn an alarm on or off without deleting it. To change one, tap its time (or tap Edit, then the alarm), make your changes and tap Done.

You can also ask Siri, for example "Set an alarm for 7 a.m." Siri adds it to the same list, so it behaves like any other alarm.

Keep a long alarm list under control

Multiple alarms go wrong when you can't tell them apart. A few habits help:

  • Label every alarm. "Work," "Gym," "Backup" or "Leave for school" tells you at a glance what each alarm is for.
  • Use Repeat instead of copies. Rather than seven alarms for seven days, make one alarm that repeats Monday to Friday and another for the weekend.
  • Give each alarm its own snooze length. Since iOS 26, the snooze isn't fixed at 9 minutes. Open an alarm, make sure Snooze is on, tap the number next to Snooze Duration and choose anything from 1 to 15 minutes. It's set per alarm, so your weekday alarm can be strict and your weekend one gentle.
  • Turn off, don't delete, the ones you use sometimes. A switched-off alarm stays in the list, ready for next time.
  • Clear out the old ones. Swipe left on an alarm and tap Delete, or tap Edit, tap the Delete (minus) button next to an alarm, then Delete.

How to turn off all alarms at once

The Clock app has no master switch, so you have three options.

1. Ask Siri

Say something like "Turn off all my alarms." Turning alarms off is different from deleting them: switched-off alarms stay in your list, so you can turn the ones you need back on later. If you want them gone for good, "Delete all my alarms" removes the whole list after you confirm. Siri's exact replies can vary between iOS versions, so open the Clock app afterwards and check.

2. Make a "Turn off alarms" Shortcut

If you clear your alarms often (for holidays, or the night before a day off), a Shortcut turns it into one tap. In the Shortcuts app, create a new shortcut with these actions in order:

  1. Get All Alarms (from the Clock actions).
  2. Repeat with Each, so the next step runs for every alarm.
  3. Toggle Alarm, inside the repeat, set to use each alarm from the repeat.

Name it something like "Turn off alarms" and add it to your Home Screen or run it with Siri. Before you rely on it, run it once and check the Clock app to make sure every alarm ended up off, not switched from off to on.

3. Do it by hand

For a short list, flicking each switch in Clock › Alarms takes a few seconds and is the most predictable option.

Do multiple alarms actually help?

They feel like a safety net, but sleep specialists aren't keen. Michelle Drerup, a behavioral sleep medicine specialist at Cleveland Clinic, points out that much of your REM sleep happens in the early morning, and repeated alarms keep interrupting it. Her advice: set your alarm for the time you really need to get up, and put the phone across the room so you have to move to stop it.

Alicia Roth, also of Cleveland Clinic, has said that more alarms can make waking up harder, not easier. If you're used to a string of them, she suggests cutting back gradually rather than all at once, and keeping the same wake time seven days a week.

A practical way to cut down:

  1. Find your real get-up time. Note when you actually get out of bed for a few days. Make that your main alarm.
  2. Keep one backup. Label it "Backup" and set it 5 to 10 minutes later. Over time, aim to never hear it.
  3. Turn the rest off, not delete. If a week goes fine, delete them.
  4. Fix the cause. If you still need five alarms, you probably need more sleep. If you're getting enough and still can't wake, talk to a doctor: sleep problems like sleep apnea and insomnia can make mornings much harder.

A note on stopping alarms in iOS 26.1

Since iOS 26.1, stopping an alarm from the Lock Screen takes a slide, while snoozing is still a single tap. That's meant to stop accidental dismissals, but it also makes snoozing the easier move. If you'd rather have a tap-to-stop button back, go to Settings › Accessibility › Touch and turn on Prefer Single-Touch Actions.

Whatever you choose, iPhone alarms still ring when Silent mode or a Focus is on.

Frequently asked questions

Can I turn off all my iPhone alarms at once?

Not with one switch in the Clock app. Ask Siri to "turn off all my alarms," or build a short Shortcut (Get All Alarms, Repeat with Each, Toggle Alarm) and check the result in the Clock app.

Is it bad to set multiple alarms?

Sleep specialists at Cleveland Clinic suggest one alarm at the time you actually need to get up, because repeated alarms break up your last stretch of sleep. If you rely on several, cut back gradually.

Can I set different alarms for weekdays and weekends?

Yes. Make one alarm that repeats Monday to Friday and another for Saturday and Sunday. Each can have its own label, sound and snooze length.
