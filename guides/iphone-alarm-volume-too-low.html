iPhone alarm volume too low? How to make it louder

6 min read · Updated November 2026 · Written for iOS 27 and iOS 26

The short version
  • iOS 27: Settings › Sounds & Haptics › under Alarms and Timers, turn off Match Ringtone Volume, then drag the new alarm slider up.
  • iOS 26: Settings › Sounds & Haptics › drag the Ringtone and Alerts slider up. That slider is your alarm volume.
  • If it keeps getting quieter on its own, check Change with Buttons, Attention Aware Features and your Sleep schedule alarm, which has its own volume.

A quiet alarm is almost as bad as no alarm. You set it, it rings, and you sleep straight through a polite little chime. The good news: on iPhone, alarm volume is nearly always a setting you can fix in a minute. The fix just depends on which version of iOS you have, because Apple changed how alarm volume works in iOS 27, released on September 14, 2026.

To check yours, open Settings › General › About and look at iOS Version. iOS 27 runs on every iPhone that runs iOS 26 (iPhone 11 and later, and the iPhone SE 2nd generation and later), so if you haven't updated yet, it's worth doing for this change alone.

1. iOS 27: give alarms their own volume

Until iOS 27, your ringtone and your alarm shared one volume. Turn calls down for a meeting and you'd quietly turn tomorrow's alarm down too. iOS 27 finally separates them.

  1. Open Settings › Sounds & Haptics.
  2. Under Alarms and Timers, turn off Match Ringtone Volume.
  3. A new slider appears. Drag it right to set how loud alarms and timers ring.

Now you can keep your ringer low, or even at zero, and still wake to a loud alarm. There's a second Match Ringtone Volume switch further down for alerts and system sounds, such as texts and keyboard clicks. That one doesn't affect alarms, so don't let it confuse you.

2. iOS 26: turn up Ringtone and Alerts

On iOS 26 the alarm has no slider of its own. It follows your ringtone volume.

  1. Open Settings › Sounds & Haptics.
  2. Drag the Ringtone and Alerts slider to the right. A sound plays as you drag, so you can hear the level.

A loud video doesn't mean a loud alarm: media volume is separate. If you press the volume buttons while watching something, you're changing media volume, not the alarm.

3. Stop the volume sliding down on its own

A very common complaint, especially on iOS 26, is setting the alarm loud and finding it quiet a few days later. The usual cause is the volume buttons. Apple's own guide says turning on Change with Buttons (in Settings › Sounds & Haptics) lets the side buttons change alarm volume. That's handy until you press volume down in a cinema, or in your pocket, and lower tomorrow's alarm without noticing.

If your alarm keeps drifting quieter, turn Change with Buttons off, set the slider where you want it, and leave it. On iOS 27, giving alarms their own slider (step 1) solves most of this too, because silencing your ringer no longer touches the alarm.

4. The alarm goes quiet when you look at it

If the alarm starts loud and fades the moment you glance at your phone, that's probably Attention Aware Features. On iPhones with Face ID, it lowers the volume of alerts when the phone can see you're looking at it. Half awake, a quieter alarm is the last thing you need.

  1. Open Settings › Accessibility › Face ID & Attention.
  2. Turn off Attention Aware Features.

5. Check your Sleep schedule alarm separately

If you set a wake-up time through Sleep in the Health app, that alarm has its own sound, volume and haptics, and the main alarm slider doesn't change it. To turn it up:

  1. Open Health › Browse › Sleep.
  2. Tap your schedule under Full Schedule & Options, then Edit.
  3. With Alarm turned on, raise the volume and pick a sound, then tap Done.

You can also change just tomorrow's wake-up alarm from the top of the Clock app's Alarms tab.

6. Get more out of the speaker

  • Pick a tone that cuts through. At the same volume, some tones are much easier to wake to than others. In Clock › Alarms, tap Edit, tap the alarm, tap Sound and preview a few with the phone across the room. Built-in tones are more reliable than songs.
  • Add vibration. On the same Sound screen, tap Haptics and choose a pattern. If the phone is on your bedside table, a buzz adds a second signal.
  • Unblock the speaker. Apple suggests removing cases and screen protectors and checking that the speaker openings aren't blocked or dirty. If needed, clean them with a small, soft, dry brush. Lint in the bottom speaker can make everything sound muffled.
  • Place it well. Lay the phone on a hard surface with the bottom speaker facing the room, not face down on a soft duvet or under a pillow, which soaks up sound.
  • Headphones are fine. Apple says that when headphones are connected, the alarm plays through the iPhone's built-in speakers as well as the headphones, so falling asleep with AirPods in won't mute it.

One honest limit: no alarm app can play louder than the volume you set in Settings. Third-party alarm apps, including ours, work within those system limits. So fix the setting first, then choose the app.

If you hear the alarm but switch it off without really waking, louder won't fix that. Upbeam's alarms ring through Silent mode and Focus, and a quick math problem has to be solved before the alarm stops, so your brain is switched on by the time it's quiet.

Test it tonight

Set an alarm for two minutes from now, lock the phone, put it where it sits overnight, and walk to your bed. If it sounds too quiet from there, it will sound quieter still when you're asleep. Adjust and test once more. Before an important morning, set a second alarm a few minutes after the first.

If it's loud enough and you still sleep through it

Some people sleep through even a full-volume alarm. Deep sleep, sleep debt and simply getting used to the same sound every morning all play a part. If that's you, the fixes are about sleep and habits as much as volume. Our guide on why you sleep through your alarm covers them. And if you're very sleepy during the day despite enough hours in bed, talk to a doctor: conditions such as sleep apnea are common and treatable.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make my iPhone alarm louder than the maximum?

No. The slider at full is as loud as the speaker goes, and alarm apps can't go beyond it. What you can change is how well the sound reaches you: a sharper tone, added vibration, a clean speaker and better placement all help.

Does Silent mode make my alarm quieter?

No. Apple says the Ring/Silent switch, Silent mode and Do Not Disturb don't affect the alarm sound. Only the volume setting in Sounds & Haptics does (and, for the Sleep alarm, its own volume in Health).

Why is my alarm loud but my calls quiet, or the other way round?

On iOS 27 with Match Ringtone Volume turned off, alarms and ringtones have separate sliders, so they can differ. On iOS 26 they share one slider, so if they sound different, check the Sleep schedule alarm, which has its own volume.
